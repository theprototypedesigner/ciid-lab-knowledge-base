# Maker resources

[← Back to the knowledge base](../README.md)

A collection of websites and tools that can help you with your projects in the lab. Most of them are free, or have a free plan that is enough for student projects.

## 3D printing

### Where to find models

These are online libraries where people share 3D models, ready to download and print. Before designing something from scratch, it is worth checking if someone already made it (or something close that you can modify).

- [Thingiverse](https://www.thingiverse.com/): one of the oldest and biggest libraries of 3D printable models.
- [Printables](https://www.printables.com/): library made by Prusa, with good quality models and print profiles for Prusa printers.
- [MakerWorld](https://makerworld.com/): library made by Bambu Lab, many models include ready-to-print settings.
- [Cults3D](https://cults3d.com/): mix of free and paid models, many design and decorative objects.
- [MyMiniFactory](https://www.myminifactory.com/): curated library, every model is tested before being published.
- [Thangs](https://thangs.com/): search engine that looks for models across many libraries at the same time, you can even search by shape.
- [GrabCAD](https://grabcad.com/library): library of CAD models shared by engineers and designers, in formats you can open in Fusion, Onshape and other CAD software (STEP, Fusion files and more). Very useful to find models of electronic components, motors or hardware to check if things fit in your design.

### Techniques

- [Watertight Prints]([https://www.thingiverse.com/](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/watertight-prints_112324)): modifying the printer settings to make an object capable of holding water without leaking

## Laser cutting

### Box and template generators

Instead of drawing every finger joint by hand, these tools generate the cutting files for you. You enter the size and the material thickness, and they give you an SVG or DXF ready to open in your design software.

- [MakerCase](https://www.makercase.com/): simple box generator with finger joints, a good place to start.
- [Boxes.py](https://boxes.hackerspace-bamberg.de/boxes.py): huge collection of generators for boxes, drawers, shelves, trays, gears and much more.
- [Templatemaker](https://www.templatemaker.nl/en/): templates for packaging and paper folding (boxes, envelopes, cones...), also useful for cardboard prototypes.

### Techniques

- [Trotec: Cutting technique for bending applications](https://www.troteclaser.com/en/helpcenter/materials/application-techniques/bending-technique): how to cut patterns (kerf bending, also called living hinges) that let stiff materials like plywood, MDF or acrylic bend.

### Design tools

- [Inkscape](https://inkscape.org/): free and open source vector design software, works well to prepare files for the laser cutter.
- [Cuttle](https://cuttle.xyz/): online design tool made for laser cutting, with parametric templates you can customise (free plan available).
- [SVGnest](https://svgnest.com/): arranges your parts on the sheet so they use as little material as possible.
- [The Noun Project](https://thenounproject.com/): icons you can use for engraving (check the licence of each icon).
- [Affinity](https://www.affinity.studio/): free vector and pixel design software. Alternative to Adobe Illustrator and Photoshop.

## 3D modelling

- [Tinkercad](https://www.tinkercad.com/): very easy 3D modelling in the browser, perfect if you have never modelled before.
- [Onshape](https://www.onshape.com/en/education/): professional CAD in the browser, free for education.
- [Autodesk Fusion](https://www.autodesk.com/education/edu-software/overview): professional CAD, free for students through the Autodesk education programme.
- [Blender](https://www.blender.org/): free and open source, great for organic shapes, sculpting and rendering.

## Electronics

- [Arduino Project Hub](https://projecthub.arduino.cc/): projects shared by the Arduino community, with code and wiring.
- [Adafruit Learning System](https://learn.adafruit.com/): very well explained tutorials about sensors, LEDs, microcontrollers and more.
- [SparkFun Tutorials](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials): tutorials covering electronics basics and their products.
- [Wokwi](https://wokwi.com/): Arduino and ESP32 simulator in the browser, test your circuit and code before building it.
- [Fritzing](https://fritzing.org/): software to draw your circuits on a breadboard, useful for documenting projects.

## CNC milling
 
### Getting started
 
- [Nomad 3 resources page](https://my.carbide3d.com/gettingstarted/nomad3/): official starting point with links to the manual, the software and the video training.
- [Nomad 3 Getting Started Guide (PDF)](https://guides.carbide3d.com/files/pdf/Nomad3_Getting_Started_Guide_12_22_2020_v1_smaller.pdf): safety, parts of the machine, first setup, setting job zero, workholding and a starter project. Read the safety section before using the machine!
- [The CNC Mindset](https://guides.carbide3d.com/start-here/the-cnc-mindset/): short introduction to how CNC milling works and how to think about it.
### Learning Carbide Create
 
- [Design with Carbide Create (video course)](https://guides.carbide3d.com/create/): official course in short videos, from job setup and drawing shapes to toolpaths, tabs and simulation. No previous experience needed.
### Learning Carbide Motion
 
Carbide 3D's video course on running the machine is made for their bigger machine, the Shapeoko, but the way you use Carbide Motion is the same on the Nomad. These are the chapters that matter most:
 
- [Machine Initialization](https://guides.carbide3d.com/running-shapeoko/machine-initialization/): connecting to the machine and homing.
- [Movements and Zeroing](https://guides.carbide3d.com/running-shapeoko/movements-and-zeroing/): moving the machine (jogging) and setting the X, Y and Z zero on your material.
- [Hello World](https://guides.carbide3d.com/running-shapeoko/hello-world/): running your first job, from the file to the finished part.
- [Hello Contour](https://guides.carbide3d.com/running-shapeoko/hello-contour/), [Hello Pocket](https://guides.carbide3d.com/running-shapeoko/hello-pocket/) and [Hello V-carve](https://guides.carbide3d.com/running-shapeoko/hello-v-carve/): small projects that show the main types of toolpaths.
Skip the chapters about BitSetter and BitZero, those are accessories for the Shapeoko.
 
### Other CNC resources
 
- [Carbide 3D manuals](https://guides.carbide3d.com/manuals/): all official manuals, including the Nomad 3 and the Nomad BitZero probe.
- [Shapeoko & Nomad feeds and speeds charts](https://community.carbide3d.com/t/shapeoko-nomad-feeds-speeds-charts/2402): community thread with recommended cutting speeds for different materials. Always start with conservative values.
- [Carbide 3D community forum](https://community.carbide3d.com/): ask questions and see what other people make with their machines.

## Materials

- [Materiom](https://commons.materiom.org/materials-database): open database of recipes to make your own bio-based materials (bioplastics, composites, foams...) from ingredients like starch, gelatine, agar or food waste.

## General inspiration

- [Instructables](https://www.instructables.com/): step by step projects of all kinds, from electronics to woodworking.

## A note on licences

Most of the files you find on these sites are shared under [Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/cclicenses/) licences. Before using a model in your project, check its licence: some allow any use, some ask you to credit the author (BY), some don't allow commercial use (NC). And if you make something nice, think about sharing it back!
